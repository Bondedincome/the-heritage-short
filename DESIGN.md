## 1. Audience
- **Primary:** my family: mom (Hule), uncle (Mehari), cousins and relatives in Addis Ababa and Gonder.
  Amharic speakers, aged roughly 12–70, who have heard this story at the dinner table.
- **Secondary:** friends and my TenX cohort, who have not heard it and may not speak Amharic (hence English subtitles).
- **Where / on what:** phones, vertical, shared in Telegram/WhatsApp family groups and on TikTok/Instagram.
  Usually watched with the sound on in the family group, sometimes muted on social, so subtitles must carry the joke alone.
- **What they already know:** the family knows the punchline by heart; outsiders know nothing about Habesha
  discipline culture or why "a lion at home" is a compliment-insult.

## 2. Intent
- **At the end they should:** laugh at the punchline, then feel the warmth underneath it: Gonder was the one
  place I felt at home enough to be the lion, and Babeye answered my punch with pride, not anger.
- **The one sentence the film must not fail to say:** "ፓፓ! ሁልዬ ወንድ ወልደሻል!", delivered clearly, with a pause before it.
- **Design consequences:** comic timing matters more than spectacle, so the punchline shot gets a beat of silence;
  the illustrated storybook style keeps the spanking/punch moment playful rather than violent; the nickname is set up
  in shot 1 and paid off in the last shot.
