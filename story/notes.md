# Story notes: "የቤት አንበሳ የዱር ጨዋ" (A lion at home, a gentleman outside)

Told by Doni (me), 26 Nov 2026. The family tells this story around the dinner table.

- **Who:** me (Doni) as a small boy; my uncle Mehari; my grandfather Babeye; my mom Hule.
- **The nickname:** my uncle called me *የቤት አንበሳ የዱር ጨዋ*, literally "a lion of the house, a gentleman of the
  wild". I caused havoc at home, but I was the quietest, politest kid at school and anywhere else that
  didn't feel like home.
- **Where:** the place that felt most like home was my grandparents' house in Gonder. We lived in Addis Ababa,
  so we flew to visit them.
- **The moment:** on one visit I caused a huge ruckus: breaking glasses and a window, yelling, a total menace.
  Babeye had had enough and came to discipline me. In Habesha homes that is often a slipper, a belt, a slap or
  a pinch; Babeye's favourite was a pinch on the inner thigh. My parents had never punished me, so I was a bit
  spoiled and didn't know what was happening. When he pinched me it hurt, and I punched him in the solar plexus.
- **The line:** he gasped and said to my mom: **"ፓፓ! ሁልዬ ወንድ ወልደሻል!"** ("Wow! Hule, you've given birth to a real man!")
- **What the family should feel at the end:** laughter at the punchline, then warmth: home was the one place I
  felt free enough to be the lion.
